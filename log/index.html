Entry II — Umbra, and the Old School coat

26 September 2026

The newest thing on the front page is Umbra: a multi-session desktop browser where every tab is its own isolated browser. Each tab gets its own cookies, storage and cache, its own proxy and exit IP, and its own fingerprint — the screen, GPU, fonts and clock set together so they agree with each other and with the network they appear on. It's for people who genuinely need many accounts, QA across regions, or want to see a page from somewhere they aren't.

Under the hood it's an Electron + React + TypeScript desktop app, with a small Go network engine doing the wire-level work and a Next.js account platform behind it. It's in beta, and it's where most of my evenings currently go. The demo on the front page is a canvas re-enactment of the core idea: five tabs open, five identities, nothing shared between them.

The site also got the coat it should have had from the start: the game's own interface. Pixel type for the chrome, bevelled stone panels, the login screen with its two fires, a quest list in red, yellow and green, a collection log with real item slots, a right-click Choose Option menu, a chatbox for game messages, and XP that levels up on the actual Old School experience table. Everything else — the duel, the charts, the NPC at the bottom — stayed exactly where it was.

Entry I — Building this place

6 July 2026

This site went from an empty folder to what you're looking at in a handful of build sessions: no frameworks, no build step — hand-written HTML, one stylesheet, and a single main.js doing everything you can see. The duel, the live charts, the quest log, the collection log: all hand-rolled canvas and vanilla JavaScript.

Some things I learned shipping it. The .gg registry publishes its zone in batches, so marcus.gg spent its first afternoon registered but invisible to the entire internet — nothing was wrong, the Channel Islands just publish on their own schedule. GitHub Pages will quietly refuse to issue an HTTPS certificate if you attach a domain before its DNS exists; removing and re-adding the domain wakes it up. And Jekyll was processing this site on every deploy for no reason at all until a .nojekyll file put a stop to it.

The fighters in the duel are modelled on my actual Old School RuneScape characters — the hood, the crest, and the twisted bow are the real loadout. If you've clicked a fighter, you've met the dragon.

The quest log on the front page is the honest ledger of what's in progress. More entries here as more things get built.
