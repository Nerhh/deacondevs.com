Admit

Ticketing for nightclubs, fight nights, grassroots sport and every independent in between, with the same class of protection as Ticketmaster’s SafeTix. A ticket is never an image: the fan’s phone signs a fresh code every ten seconds, and one scan lets one person in.

What it stops

Screenshots and photocopies
Codes expire within about twenty seconds, and the moving strip and live clock give a still image away at a glance.
Codes worked out in advance
Anything more than one step ahead is refused.
Forged or edited codes
Signing needs the phone’s private key, made on that phone as non-extractable: JavaScript, ours included, can ask it to sign but can never read it out.
One ticket, two doors
A single conditional database write admits a ticket. Two doors scanning at the same instant can’t both win; it’s tested against real Postgres.
Reselling after a transfer
A transfer re-keys the ticket, so the seller’s codes die the moment the buyer accepts.
A lost phone
Removing it from the account stops its codes at every door at once.
A stolen scanner
Doors hold public keys only. A scanner can check tickets but never mint one.

Where it stops. A door working offline from its downloaded list can still hold a seller’s old key after a transfer, and admit them once; the sync flags any second entry. So on events that use offline doors, transfers close before doors open.

About

Organisers run everything from Studio: events, price brackets and sections, rules, door setup and live check-ins, guest lists and a box office, discount and presale codes, refunds, payouts, waitlists and face-value resale. Fans buy at all-in prices on an event page that embeds in any website, and keep their tickets in a wallet that installs from the browser.

Any phone, or a handheld USB or Bluetooth scanner, becomes a door with a six-character pairing code, and shows green or red with the guest’s seat. The rules are the organiser’s: transfers on or off, a transfer cut-off, tickets per person, an age limit, re-entry. Pro adds phone lock, offline doors and staged code release.

The whole product runs on a laptop with no accounts, against stand-ins for payments and email. What stands between the build and selling real tickets is the business side: a payments account, a domain, a solicitor’s review and an independent penetration test. Admit is a working name.

Lifetime
About twenty seconds: one step of grace either side
Entry
One conditional write per scan; the first scan wins
Stack
Hono API on Node, Postgres (embedded PGlite locally), React and Vite
Tests
412 end-to-end security tests that attack the real API; Playwright layout, accessibility and flow suites
Load
Twenty doors at five scans a second each: p95 6 ms on one desktop, no double admission
Status
Pre-launch: built and running end to end; launch waits on the business’s accounts and legal sign-off
